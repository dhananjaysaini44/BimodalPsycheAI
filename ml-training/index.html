1. Project Overview & Research Scope

Benchmarking classical machine learning, transfer representations, and lightweight attention on clinical depression screening

BimodalPsycheAI conducts an empirical benchmark of objective depression detection on the Extended Distress Analysis Interview Corpus (E-DAIC / AVEC 2019). The benchmark rigorously evaluates two complementary communicative modalities:

  • Voice / Audio Modality: 100 Hz acoustic Low-Level Descriptors (eGeMAPS 23-d, MFCC 39-d), 10 Hz Bag-of-Audio-Words (BoAW 100-d codebooks), deep CNN transfer representations (VGG16 4,096-d and DenseNet201 1,920-d), neural MLP heads, and unidirectional GRU temporal sequence models with masked attention.
  • Conversational Text Modality: Participant transcript n-gram TF-IDF baselines, dense frozen sentence embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 384-d), chronological interview segmentation (early, middle, late, concatenated), frozen transformer chunk representations (distilbert-base-uncased 768-d), and lightweight trainable additive attention pooling.

1. Conversational Text Outperforms Acoustic Features

Across both depression classification (binary PHQ ≥ 10) and continuous severity tracking (PHQ score 0–24), conversational text representations consistently achieve higher concordance and lower prediction errors than acoustic descriptors:

  • Classification: Text models achieve Dev F1 up to 0.5161 and ROC-AUC of 0.7159, compared to peak acoustic F1 of 0.4286 (VGG16) and 0.4000 (Raw MFCC).
  • Regression: DistilBERT fixed pooling achieves Dev CCC of 0.4048 with RMSE of 5.34, significantly surpassing peak audio regression (MFCC Ridge CCC = 0.2844, RMSE = 6.22).

2. Attention Pooling: High ROC-AUC but Severe Regression Collapse

Phase 18C and its independent forensic audit revealed an intriguing dichotomy for lightweight additive attention pooling over frozen DistilBERT chunk representations:

  • Classification: Trainable attention preserves strong rank discrimination (ROC-AUC = 0.7121–0.7348 across seeds, higher than fixed pooling's 0.6591).
  • Regression: Attention pooling suffers from severe variance collapse (prediction σ = 2.45 vs target σ = 6.01, a 63% compression), driving Dev CCC down to 0.0723 (3-seed mean: 0.0466).

5. Voice / Audio Modality Deep Dive

Analysis of acoustic Low-Level Descriptors, BoAW codebooks, deep CNN embeddings, and temporal sequence models

The audio benchmark evaluates whether acoustic vocal dynamics alone can reliably distinguish depression or track clinical severity on conversational speech:

  • Classical Acoustic LLDs: Raw MFCC mean_std (78-d) with LinearSVC achieves Dev F1=0.4000 and ROC-AUC=0.6761; Ridge regression achieves Dev CCC=0.2844. eGeMAPS descriptors exhibit substantially lower sensitivity (F1=0.2222, CCC=0.0273).
  • Bag-of-Audio-Words (BoAW): Codebook quantization (100 bins at 10 Hz) with XGBoost achieves lower RMSE (5.7172) and MAE (4.6654) than Ridge regression, but shows lower linear tracking (CCC=0.1613).
  • Pretrained CNN Transfer Learning: VGG16 embeddings (4,096-d) achieve peak audio classification F1 of 0.4286 (ROC-AUC=0.6477). DenseNet201 embeddings achieve CCC=0.1496 with Ridge and 0.1654 with a neural MLP prediction head.
  • Temporal Sequence Modeling (GRU): Window-level GRU with temporal attention pooling retains moderate ROC-AUC (0.6648) but suffers from threshold collapse at standard decision cutoffs, resulting in F1=0.0000.

6. Conversational Text Modality Deep Dive

From sparse n-gram TF-IDF to transformer embeddings, chronological segmentation, and additive attention

Text experiments progressed across five formal phases (Phases 17A through 18C):

  1. Phase 17A (TF-IDF Baselines): Character n-gram TF-IDF with calibrated LinearSVC established a strong benchmark baseline (Dev F1=0.4571, ROC-AUC=0.7159; Ridge CCC=0.3211, RMSE=4.9812).
  2. Phase 17B & 17B.1 (MiniLM Dense Embeddings): Frozen all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings established dense semantic baselines (Dev F1=0.4000, CCC=0.3057), which were systematically audited and tuned.
  3. Phase 18A (Chronological Segmentation): Partitioning transcripts into early, middle, and late segments showed that the middle segment (where Ellie probes mood, relationships, and sleep) yields peak classification performance (F1=0.5161, BalAcc=0.7083).
  4. Phase 18B (DistilBERT Embeddings & Fixed Pooling): Attention-mask-aware mean pooling over frozen DistilBERT chunks produced the highest regression agreement in the project: whole__mean_std achieved Dev CCC=0.4048 (RMSE=5.3423) and middle__mean achieved CCC=0.4022.
  5. Phase 18C (Trainable Attention Pooling): Adding a 2-layer MLP additive attention pooling head retained high ROC-AUC (0.7121–0.7348 across seeds) but caused significant target variance compression in regression, failing to beat fixed pooling.

7. Research Methodology & Scientific Limitations

Participant-level isolation, cross-validation discipline, and clinical benchmark constraints

Participant-Level Cross-Validation Hygiene

All chunk embeddings, acoustic frames, and feature tables are grouped strictly by participant identifier. Chunks or frames from the same individual never cross train and validation folds. Standard scalers and decision thresholds are fitted exclusively on training folds.

Test Set Quarantine Guarantee

The official Test split (n=56 participants) remains 100% quarantined. Zero test labels or test predictions are displayed on this dashboard or used for model tuning. Test evaluation is reserved strictly for the designated final release phase.

Evaluation Cohort Transparency

3-segment concatenation requires all three interview phases (early, middle, late), resulting in a matched cohort of 54 Development participants (10 depressed). Whole-interview models evaluate all 56 participants (12 depressed). Cohort sizes are explicitly distinguished in all tables.

Why CCC ≠ Pearson Correlation

Pearson's r measures linear association but is invariant to shift and scale errors. Lin's Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) penalizes both slope deviation and mean bias against the identity line (y = ŷ), making it the gold standard for clinical severity agreement.
